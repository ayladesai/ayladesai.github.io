About Me

Hi! I’m Ayla, a sophomore mechanical engineering student at Cornell University. Originally from San Diego, California, I’m a lifelong tinkerer with interests that range across aerospace, robotics, and automotive engineering.

I am currently a member of the Cornell Baja Racing project team, a top-ranking group that designs, builds, and races a new off-road vehicle each year in the Baja SAE collegiate series. For the 2026-2027 season, I am responsible for master cylinder design and fabrication for the brakes subteam. Last year I was involved in seat fabrication; U-joint cube manufacturing; and firewall, spillguard, and non-structural tab design and assembly.

In high school, I was a founding member of the FIRST Tech Challenge (FTC) team Definitely Human, which peaked in global rankings at #2 in autonomous and #6 overall. I was also selected for the Navy’s Science and Engineering Apprenticeship Program (SEAP) during the summer of 2024, spending 8 weeks at NIWC Pacific headquarters researching antifouling ship hull coatings with Channing Bolt’s research group.

In my free time I love to build things useful and silly alike. For my parents’ biology lab I developed a 3D-printed tool to help manipulate tiny (~1mm) nematode worms, replacing previous makeshift solutions. For my family’s annual pie competition, my uncle and I once rigged up an automatic whipped cream dispenser to compensate for our pretty unimpressive entry.

To me, engineering has always been a vehicle for exploration, progress, and fun. On this site, I’ve compiled more detailed descriptions of a few of my most relevant engineering projects. If you are interested in learning more about any of this work or have any questions for me, please don’t hesitate to reach out by email! I always love to get into the weeds, exactly where I belong.
